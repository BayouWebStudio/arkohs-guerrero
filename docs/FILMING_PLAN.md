# Sacred Geometry Masterclass — Filming Plan

**Target: ~50 lessons · ~9.5 hours of finished video · 11 modules**

This plan matches `content/curriculum.ts` (which drives the sales page), so if you
change a lesson here, change it there too.

---

## The four formats (film by format, not by module)

Batching by setup is the single biggest time-saver. You'll set up each rig once
and knock out every lesson that uses it.

| Format | Setup | Lessons | Est. shoot time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Talking head** | You on camera, studio backdrop, key light, lav or shotgun mic | 13 lessons | 2 full days |
| **B. Overhead desk cam** | Camera mounted above your drawing table, paper + compass visible, voiceover live or after | 6 lessons | 1 day |
| **C. iPad screen capture** | iPad screen recording + small picture-in-picture of you (optional), narrated | 15 lessons | 2–3 days |
| **D. Tattoo session** | 2 cameras at a real session (wide + macro on the skin), client release signed | 14 lessons | 2–3 client sessions |
| **E. Documentary** | Mixed: b-roll, interviews, time-lapse — for trailer, consultation, capstone | 3 pieces | shot across everything else |

**Rule of thumb for lesson length:** teaching lessons 8–15 min, demos 12–20 min,
nothing over 20 except the capstone. Attention drops hard after 15 minutes;
two 10-minute lessons always beat one 20-minute lesson.

---

## Gear checklist

- **Camera:** any Fuji body you own is more than enough (X100-series works for
  talking head; an X-T body with a 35mm or 56mm equivalent is ideal). 4K/24 or 25.
- **Second angle for sessions:** phone on a clamp arm is fine for the macro shot,
  or a second body with a macro-capable lens.
- **Audio matters more than image.** A $150–300 wireless lav (DJI Mic / Rode
  Wireless GO) will do more for perceived quality than any lens. Record backup
  audio on the camera.
- **Light:** one soft key light (Amaran/Godox ~$150) + whatever ambient you have.
- **Overhead rig:** desk mount arm (~$40) pointing straight down; lock exposure
  and focus manually so it doesn't hunt while you draw.
- **iPad capture:** built-in iOS screen recording is fine; AirDrop files off after
  each lesson. Record narration separately on the lav for clean audio.

## Suggested shooting order (≈ 2–3 weeks of half-days)

1. **Week 1:** All iPad screen captures (Modules 2–4 design lessons) — no crew, no
   client, just you. Builds momentum. Then the overhead desk lessons (Module 1, 0.3, 6.1).
2. **Week 2:** Talking-head batch — write bullet outlines (never scripts), film all
   13 in two sittings while the lighting is set up.
3. **Weeks 2–3:** Client sessions. You need **2–3 booked clients** who consent to
   filming (discount their session in exchange). Capture Modules 5–7 + the capstone
   thread across these sessions. Always run both cameras; storage is cheap, reshoots aren't.
4. **Last:** Cut the trailer (0.1) from the best of everything.

## Production rules

- **Bullet points, not scripts.** You explain this stuff to apprentices already —
  outline 5 beats per lesson on an index card and talk.
- **Every construction lesson ships a worksheet PDF** (the construction steps as
  a printable). Cheap to make, massively raises perceived value.
- **Film every session in 4K even if you publish 1080p** — lets you punch in for
  "macro" shots in the edit without a second camera.
- **Get signed video releases from every client on camera.** One-page form, before
  the session starts.
- **Name files as you go:** `M06_L03_straight-lines_camA_take2.mov`. Future you
  will be grateful.
